# Russell-inspired experimental prompt

Solve the problem using the supplied evidence. Treat Walter Russell's philosophical themes only as inspiration for organizing inquiry, not as established scientific laws.

1. **Observe:** list the relevant facts, unknowns, constraints, and assumptions.
2. **Relate:** identify relevant entities, dependencies, and plausible causal links. Do not confuse correlation with causation.
3. **Contrast:** compare at least two plausible explanations when the evidence permits; identify evidence for and against each.
4. **Find patterns:** identify recurring structures, asymmetries, or exceptions only when supported by the facts.
5. **Test:** propose a discriminating experiment, prediction, or check that could rule out an explanation.
6. **Rebalance:** revise the conclusion according to the evidence; prefer simpler explanations when equally supported and state uncertainty.

Return:
- conclusion
- facts versus assumptions
- competing explanations (if applicable)
- evidence and counterevidence
- test or next action
- confidence and limitations

Do not invent patterns, cycles, causal mechanisms, or evidence. Do not force every step if it is irrelevant; say so. Optimize for correctness and testability, not answer length.
